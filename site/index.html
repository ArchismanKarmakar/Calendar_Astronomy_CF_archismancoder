The night sky of 2027, on your wall.

A wall and desk calendar for astronomers and astrophotographers. Eclipses, meteor showers, comets and dark-sky nights are computed for your city, under photographs from ESO, ESA/Hubble, ESA/Webb and NOIRLab.

    Every night, rated for the Moon

    Each date shows how much of the Moon is lit at 9 pm. Dark nights are tinted, so galaxy and nebula sessions can be planned at a glance.

    Times to the minute

    Eclipses as seen from your city, meteor peaks, oppositions, conjunctions and occultations, in local time with daylight saving.

    A target every month

    The photograph's own galaxy or nebula, and the time it stands highest from where you are.

      Questions

      How do I print it?

      Print at 100% ("actual size") on the paper named in the file: A3 or Tabloid for the wall calendar, A5 or half-letter for the desk calendar. The PDFs include bleed so a print shop can trim to the edge. On a home printer, choose "fit to page".

      How accurate are the dates and times?

      Moon phases, planets, conjunctions and occultations are computed with established astronomical models and are usually accurate to within a minute or two. Eclipse times come from NASA. Meteor-shower peaks follow the International Meteor Organization's 2027 calendar. Comet brightness is a forecast and can change. Holidays set by moon sighting are marked as estimates.

      Can I use my own photograph?

      Yes. In "Make your own", tap any month's picture and choose "Your photo". The photo is read in your browser and never leaves your device. You need to own it, or have permission to print it.

      Can I print it for my club, or sell it?

      Printing for yourself, as a gift, or for a school or astronomy club to hand out is welcome. This is a free, non-commercial project, so please don't sell copies or re-host the files without asking first. The photographs keep their own licences. The terms have the details.

      Why does the first build take a while?

      The calendar is drawn on your device rather than on a server. The first time, your browser downloads the drawing engine (about 40 MB) and the full-size photographs. Later builds reuse them.
